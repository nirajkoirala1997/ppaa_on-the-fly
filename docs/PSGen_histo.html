PSGen_histo

What the phase space used to be and what it is now, and how the distributions came to be filled inside the run that produces the cross section. Every claim on this page is quoted from the tree at ba6cb4f plus the uncommitted working copy.

What this build is

Leading-order Drell–Yan, p p → e+ e− at 13.6 TeV. Phase space comes from PSGen (Kolodziej), used as a mapping and nothing more. The integration loop belongs to VEGAS (vsup.f), which therefore sees and adapts every integration variable. Amplitudes come from OpenLoops, parton densities from LHAPDF (PDF4LHC21_40). One run is one seed; a campaign is several seeds merged by summing raw accumulators.

Why PSGen and not KALEU

This is the decision the whole arrangement follows from, so it is worth stating before anything else. KALEU owns its own integration loop: it draws its own random numbers and adapts the variables it knows about. A variable that is not part of phase space — a Mellin-inversion contour parameter, say — is invisible to it and can never be adapted. PSGen draws nothing. It consumes x(1:ndim) and hands back momenta, so VEGAS keeps the loop and keeps seeing every dimension, phase-space or not.

Read this correctly

There is no Mellin machinery in this tree. The comment above is the argument for the design, not a description of code that exists. No contour, no N-space variable, no minimal prescription. The whole point is that yy(5) is available when it is wanted.

PSGen is a mapping, not an integrator

psgen() never draws a random number. It takes x(1:ndim) in (0,1) and returns the momenta, the initial-state flux factor and the phase-space normalisation. Its own multichannel integrator, carlos.f, is deliberately not linked — at nkin = 1 it would have nothing to adapt, and linking it would put a second sampler in the loop.

The linked set is the 27 sources reachable at nkin = 1: psgen → kincls → kinschnl → kin2_2 → kinff, plus psncls, kinin, param_trans and the cut and index bookkeeping. Seventy-four higher-multiplicity kinematics and helpers, together with carlos.f and cs_sect.f, sit in LO_check_DY/.psgen_unused/; PSGen_1.1/ is kept so more channels can be regenerated. The generated process is fixed at u u~ → e+ e− with nexp = 4, nfsp = 2, nkin = 1.

Before

At f2271db the kinematics were hand-written. kinvar2 builds a 2→2 configuration directly in the lab frame from a single variable v = xx(3), generates no azimuth at all (p3(2) is identically zero), and carries no explicit Jacobian — the whole dv dφ factor was folded into a constant xnorm downstream.

PSGen was in the file at that commit, but wired experimentally and never allowed to run: the beam fractions were re-read out of yy(3) and yy(4), the third PSGen variable came from yy(5), ndim was passed as 3 for a two-body mapping, and the block ended in a debug dump and a bare stop. The flavour sum was d and s only.

After — the wrapper and its three traps

Everything psgen_interface exists to absorb is a sharp edge in psgen.f itself. All three are real, all three are silent, and each one would produce plausible wrong numbers rather than a crash.

Trap 1 · flux goes stale

psgen assigns flux only when the partonic energy differs from the one it saw last (psgen.f:48, ecm0 is saved). On a repeat it leaves the caller’s variable untouched. Upstream cs_sect.f copes with a save flux; the wrapper does the same with flux_saved.

Trap 2 · dlips is not zeroed on failure

When kincls fails, psgen skips its entire body (psgen.f:60) and leaves dlips holding whatever the caller had in it — which, in a loop, is the previous point’s value. Zero it first, always.

Trap 3 · fourmom::p is global, and partonic-CM

The momenta live in a module-level array that the next call overwrites, and they are in the partonic centre-of-mass frame. Copy out immediately, then boost to the lab with the same transform psgen uses internally when it imposes cuts.

What psgen_init switches off, and why

The one-shot initialisation is mostly a list of PSGen defaults that would quietly change the physics. The most consequential is icuts = 0: PSGen’s define_cuts.f ships with a 5° lepton–beam cut and an E_lep > 5 GeV cut enabled. It must be set before param_trans, because define_cuts branches on it.

Stale comment

“the mass window is closed by the tau sampling instead” is not true of this build. There is no tau sampling here — x1 and x2 are flat and the window is a rejection cut on the reconstructed m(ee). The sentence describes the sibling KALEU code. Same for the “tau/y sampling” note on psgen_event.

Where the two random numbers actually go

psgen_ndim = 3*nfsp - 4, which is 2 here, and both are consumed at the bottom of the chain in kinff. rand(1) becomes the polar angle, rand(2) the azimuth. The shifted forms — ct = 2·rand(1) with pp*(ct-1) rather than ct = 2·rand(1)-1 with pp*ct — are a numerical-stability rewrite; the original lines are still there, commented out directly above.

The rest of the weight is assembled on the way back up. This is the arithmetic worth checking by hand once, because it is what makes the switch away from kinvar2 verifiable rather than merely plausible.

The integrand, in full

Short enough to read whole, and every non-obvious line carries its reason. Three choices in here are deliberate and would each be tempting to “fix”: flat beam fractions, the mass window taken from rebuilt momenta rather than x1 x2 s, and the absence of muR.

→Why the beam fractions stay flat

A tau = x1 x2 mapping measures about 25× better on the Monte Carlo error at equal statistics. It was not taken, because the sampling then becomes something the integrand has to get right separately for each multiplicity. Keeping x1, x2 raw is what lets this integrand carry over to the real emission unchanged — and VEGAS adapts those two dimensions anyway, which recovers part of what the mapping would have bought.

→Why muR is missing rather than unused

The tree amplitude carries no power of gs, so nothing in the integrand is allowed to depend on the renormalisation scale. xmur is not imported at all — it is carried elsewhere for labelling only. muF is taken from m(e+e−) rather than sqrt(ŝ): the same number here, different the moment a gluon appears, and this is the one that stays right.

The integrator PSGen is driven by

PSGen’s role only makes sense against what VEGAS is doing around it. The grid holds at most NDMX = 50 bins per axis with damping exponent ALPH = 1.5, and MDS = 1 means importance sampling and stratification. Two passes: ISTAT 1/3 refines the grid, ISTAT 2 resets the cumulants so nothing from the first pass survives, ISTAT 4 integrates on the frozen grid.

What survives the addition of a gluon

  • xa, xb are handed over as they come, so the beam side is unchanged.
  • The mass window is applied to m(ee) rebuilt from momenta, so it stays correct when ŝ and m(ee)² part company.
  • muF is built from m(ee) for the same reason.
  • Only the leg count passed to analysis_fill and the value of psgen_ndim change — and psgen_ndim is 3*nfsp - 4, so it changes itself.

Before, in two stages

These were different situations and it is worth not collapsing them.

01f2271db — one full VEGAS run per mass point

The driver scanned an invariant mass xq in steps, running the entire integration from scratch at each step, and the integrand rejected every event outside a ±5 GeV slice around it. A distribution cost one adaptation and one production pass per point, every event outside the slice was discarded, and each point’s error came from a different VEGAS run.

022006b63 — no distributions at all

After the strip, the entire output of a run was three numbers.

The negative, stated explicitly

There was no event dump, no ntuple, no LHE file and no post-processing step — nothing to re-analyse afterwards. PSGen ships a distribs.f, but it is not in PSGEN_OBJS and has never been compiled into this build.

After — two hooks, at the iteration boundary

The whole change on the integrator side is that the pass sizes became arguments and two calls appeared. The production pass used to be hardcoded as 10*npt points and 2*its iterations, which made the statistics behind a plot something you had to read the integrator to know.

The hooks have to be here, at the iteration boundary and outside vegas_m, because one iteration is the sample unit for every histogram error bar. They are plain external subroutines rather than module procedures so that fixed-form F77 never has to know how the compiler mangles module names.

Until the first hook fires, the fill is a no-op. That is what lets the integrand call analysis_fill unconditionally and say nothing about which pass it is in — and it is what guarantees adaptation points, drawn from a density that is still moving, never reach a bin.

The statistics — why an iteration, not an event

This is the one thing that does not carry over from the KALEU code, and getting it wrong would produce a plausible number with a wrong error bar. KALEU draws independent points, so it can take the per-point spread as the error. VEGAS cannot: with MDS = 1 the points inside one iteration are stratified — deliberately anti-correlated — and their spread overstates the variance of their sum. What is independent is one iteration from the next, which is already the estimator vsup uses for the total.

Three arrays per histogram carry that: hcur accumulates during an iteration, hw and hww are folded once at the boundary, nit counts the folds. Because VEGAS calls the integrand with vwgt = WGT/ITMX, the 1/ITMX is already inside every weight, so hw comes out as the mean directly rather than as a sum needing division.

The mean and the error are functions rather than inlined algebra so that the report writer, the console summary and anything else wanting the numbers agree on the estimator by construction instead of by copied arithmetic. It is also why it_prod >= 2 is enforced by the card reader: with one iteration there is no spread to quote.

The total, by the same machinery

htot is a one-bin histogram of unit width that every accepted event falls into. That is not a trick to save code — it is what guarantees the quoted total and the bin contents cannot drift apart through two copies of the same algebra. It also means histo and sum_w in the report are numerically identical, which is a useful thing to know when reading a file.

The run therefore ends with two estimators of one number. VEGAS quotes a variance-weighted average over iterations, with a chi²/it saying whether that weighting was legitimate; the histograms quote a plain unweighted mean, because a weighted one cannot be merged bin by bin across runs. They must agree well inside the error, and main.f90 prints the pull every run.

Which distributions are exact in this build

The integrand sums both beam orderings — quark from beam 1, quark from beam 2 — against a single amplitude evaluation. The exchange flips the sign of cosθ*, the lepton angle in the dilepton rest frame measured from the quark direction. So the shortcut is exact for any observable even in cosθ* and wrong in the odd part, which is the forward–backward asymmetry.

StatusObservables
exactm(ee) · pt of either lepton · pt(ll) · y(ll) · dphi(ll)

The eta and dr rows ship commented out for that reason, and it is the same reason no angular acceptance cut was added — adding one means evaluating the two orderings separately first.

Closure checks that come for free
  • pt(ll) puts all its weight in bin 1, with sum_w equal to the run total to the last digit — at leading order the dilepton system has no recoil. Anything else means the two-body kinematics or the boost to the lab frame is wrong.
  • dphi(ll) lands entirely in the π bin.
  • pt(e+) equals pt(e−) bin by bin.
  • m(ee) peaks in the 91 GeV bin.

Why the card is keyed

The old format was positional, and said so itself.

Inserting, removing or reordering a line silently shifted every subsequent value into the wrong variable, and the only defence was a convention about appending. The keyed parser turns three classes of mistake into hard errors instead of wrong numbers: an unknown key (caught by card_report_unused once every getter has run), a missing required key (caught by the card_require_* getters), and a duplicate key (caught at parse time, with both line numbers reported).

Each entry stores its section, its key, its value, the line it came from and a used flag; [histograms] rows carry a name as well, so one section can hold repeated definitions. Lookup folds case on both sides, so a caller asking for mZ finds the stored mz rather than silently falling back to a default. At the end, card_dump echoes every parsed entry into the report, which is what makes a report plus its echoed card a complete description of the run.

Every key

[run] seed
Positive integer, required. Overridden by the first command-line argument. One run is one seed; independent statistics come from several seeds merged, not from one long job.
[run] n_adapt, it_adapt
Adaptation pass. These points are thrown away — they are not histogrammed and do not enter the quoted cross section, because they are drawn from a density that is still moving.
[run] n_prod, it_prod
Production pass, grid frozen. it_prod is not cosmetic: it is the number of sample units behind every error bar in the report, so it wants to be at least about 10. Values below 2 are rejected.
[output] dir, file
dir is overridden by the second command-line argument. file names the merged report; a single run is always written seed-tagged.
[beam] ecm
Hadronic centre-of-mass energy in GeV. Also the fallback upper edge of the mass window.
[pdf] set, member
LHAPDF set name and member. This build evaluates one member only.
[scales] dynamic, mur_fixed, muf_fixed
dynamic = yes gives muR = muF = m(e+e−) event by event. muR is inert at this order — the tree amplitude carries no power of gs, so nothing in the integrand is allowed to read it. Only muF moves the answer; mur_fixed is kept so a run can still be labelled.
[masses] mZ, wZ, mW, wW, mH, mtop
Handed to OpenLoops through constants_mod. wZ is physics, not bookkeeping: the Z propagator is resonant in the middle of the generated range and OpenLoops runs in the complex-mass scheme built from exactly these numbers. wZ = 0 is rejected.
[ew] gfermi
alpha is derived from this through the Gmu relation in constants_mod::alpha_gfermi and handed to OpenLoops explicitly (ew_scheme = 0) rather than left for OpenLoops to rederive — the two differ by about 0.1 %.
[cuts] mee_min, mee_max
The only selection this build applies. mee_min is not optional: the leptons are massless and the amplitude contains gamma* → e+ e−, so the integrand diverges as 1/m(ee)² and a zero here gives a meaningless cross section rather than a large one. mee_max = 0 means no upper edge and resolves to ecm.
[histograms] <name> obs / leg / legs / nbins / xmin / xmax
One row per distribution. Events outside [xmin,xmax] are simply not binned; they still count towards the total cross section, which makes a histogram range a plotting choice rather than a cut.

The mee1 / mee2 / mee4 / mee7 / mee10 family is the same distribution at 1, 2, 4, 7 and 10 GeV bin widths over the same range — rebinning decided at run time rather than afterwards. ptll is the free check described above.

The command line, and why it is only two arguments

Seed tagging is the merge guard

Every report is written as LO_<seed>.dat. That is not decoration: merge_runs.py combines a campaign by globbing LO_*.dat, and the merged result lands on the untagged LO.dat, which the glob cannot match. A merged report therefore can never be fed back into its own merge and have its points counted twice. The naming is the guard.

tools/run_seeds.sh — and the seed study behind it

tools/run_seeds.sh <nseeds> [njobs] reads the card rather than duplicating it, builds an explicit seed list so nothing globs, fans the jobs out with GNU parallel or xargs -P, judges each job by whether its file appeared, and merges whatever succeeded. A failed seed does not abort the campaign, but the exit status is non-zero so a wrapper can notice.

Seeds are 1001, 1002, 1003, … — the run index, as in the KALEU tree. That convention was checked rather than inherited, because this build’s generator is BRM48 (RANMAR), not the RANLUX the KALEU tree seeds. BRM48I splits its seed into four numbers and fills its 97-word table from a recurrence in three of them, so every seed below 30082 lands in the same cell and consecutive seeds share most of that recurrence. On paper that is a weak corner of RANMAR’s seeding.

tools/merge_runs.py

Merging pools the per-iteration estimates over every iteration of every run. Each report carries, per bin, hw = ∑u_i and hww = ∑u_i² over n = niter; since u_i already carries the 1/ITMX, the independent estimates are v_i = n·u_i.

Applied to a single run this reproduces that run’s own numbers exactly, which is the identity test worth running after any change. Unlike averaging per-run means it stays correct when runs contribute unequal numbers of iterations. A companion repack() writes back the hw/hww a reader recovers the same mean and error from, so a merged report obeys the same file contract as a single run.

Two different combinations in one file

The histogram columns pool accumulators as above. The VEGAS column cannot be pooled that way — VEGAS quotes a variance-weighted average over its own iterations, and each seed adapted its own grid, so the seeds are independent measurements of the same number with individually meaningful errors. That column uses an inverse-variance combination instead. Which is why chi2_per_it in a merged file is the chi²/dof of the seeds, not of one run’s iterations — and the merger writes that caveat into the file itself.

What the merge refuses: runs with different npoints_per_iter are not mergeable, however tempting, because a v_i’s variance depends on how many points went into its iteration. Same for a different ecm, PDF, cut, scale, or physics card — those are different quantities. The check goes beyond the header: card_signature() compares the physics half of the echoed card, dropping [run] and [output], because two runs at different mZ have identical headers. A crashed or truncated seed, by contrast, is merely skipped, because one dead job must not cost the campaign.

What merging cannot fix

Summing accumulators is exact but blind. A seed whose VEGAS grid adapted badly still contributes its full weight, drags the mean and shrinks the pooled error — a confidently quoted wrong number. The per-seed pull table and the chi²/dof printed at the end are what expose that, and they are the reason to run a campaign rather than one long job. Above chi²/dof = 3 the merger says so explicitly.

tools/extract_data.py

The two arguments may be given either way round, since the KALEU tree’s version takes <what> first and that habit should not cost a rerun. A distribution can be named exactly, by case-insensitive prefix, or by alias against the recorded xlabel. Columns are read by name from the block’s # columns: line, not by position. Output is bin centre, dσ/d(observable) in fb per unit of that observable, and its error — multiply by the bin width for the cross section in a bin.

The --scales and --with-pdf flags exist already and currently print a note and give the central column anyway. They are written against the extension contract (# nvar = N counting the variations, # nscale = M saying how many of the leading ones are the scale grid) so that the reader and the Fortran side stay in step when the 7-point variation lands.

The report format

Four block kinds, all line-oriented, all with # comments carrying the metadata. Everything a merge step needs to check that two files describe the same calculation lives in the first block; the echoed card carries the rest.

Points per iteration and the iteration count are recorded separately rather than only as their product, because the iteration is the sample unit for every error in the file. Cuts and scales travel with the data rather than only in the echoed card, because runs made under different selections or scales describe different quantities and must never be merged. And in every histogram block the physical values come first with the raw accumulators trailing, so a plotting script keeps the column positions it expects.
